What the marbles stand for

A colour = a kind of code pattern

Some patterns are common, a few are rare. The jar starts with 16 of the most common colour and a single lime marble.

Picking at random = training on its own output

The next jar only contains what was picked from the last one. A colour that is missed once is gone for good.

Fresh marbles = real data

Adding marbles from the original jar keeps rare colours in play, so the jar stays varied for much longer.

What happened in the real experiment

6.2 → 5.3bits of code-pattern variety for purely synthetic training, over ten generations
4.7bits at the lowest point for syntax-filtered synthetic training (generation 6)
≈ 6.2bits with half the training data kept real, steady the whole way

This is a toy model. Real training is messier than drawing marbles from a jar, but the effect is the same shape: rare things vanish first, and without new real data they do not come back. In the study the same pattern ended in code that stopped working by generation 4.
